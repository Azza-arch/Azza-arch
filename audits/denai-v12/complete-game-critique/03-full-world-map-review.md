Method: independent re-audit, not inherited from prior phase reports. A disposable copy of the game (`app/addmath/rooftop/` copied to a scratch directory, served on a separate port, with a query-free debug hook added only to that disposable copy) was used to position the camera/player directly for screenshot capture. **This is fixture positioning, not natural play** — disclosed per the brief's instructions. The real working copy at `app/addmath/rooftop/` was never modified (verified: `grep qaScene app/addmath/rooftop/src/v12/game.js` returns nothing after this audit). Evidence: `screenshots/strip-01-x0-2000.jpg` through `strip-05-x7000-9200.jpg` (zoom 0.62, overlapping, covering the full 0–8400px world in room order) plus `screenshots/r01-*.jpg` through `r16-*.jpg` (one targeted shot per room, zoom 1). Left-boundary: `r01-langkah-pertama.jpg` and `strip-01`. Mid-world: `r08/r09/r10` and `strip-03`. Right-boundary: `r16-pintu-destinasi.jpg`, `strip-05`.

## What the five strips show, in order

**Strip 1 (x≈0–2000, Zone A → early B).** A single large tree landmark, a crate-and-switch puzzle on a long flat run, a gap, then the first of many identical "two-post-and-a-beam-with-a-ladder" timber modules starting the B climb. The visual language of the entire game is already established in this first screen: brown dirt/green-grass land masses (`kppLandMassFill`-style, per source) at a few different flat tiers, connected by the same repeated support module. Nothing here reads as "wrong," but nothing reads as *this specific place* either — it is the general-purpose Kenney platformer vocabulary, used competently but without a single invented motif unique to DENAI yet (the one tree is the single exception, and the source comments confirm this was a deliberate, singular placement).

**Strip 2 (x≈1600–3800, B climb → lift → D entry).** This is the most structurally repetitive stretch in the game. Counting distinct post+ladder+deck assemblies visible in one screen: at least five near-identical instances (two in the B climb, the lift's own frame, one more before D, one at D's rack). They are not *wrong* — each is individually load-bearing and legible — but stacked this close together with identical proportions, identical plank color, and identical post spacing, they read as the same prefab copy-pasted, which is exactly the "repeated rectangular geometry" and "awkward architectural support structures" complaint in the owner's brief. The lift mechanism itself (visible mid-strip) is the one cluster with real distinguishing detail (actuator marks, a lever, a selector) — it stands out specifically *because* everything around it doesn't.

**Strip 3 (x≈3400–5600, D landing → full E waterworks).** The clearest "mechanism silhouette" in the game: the blue pipe run and vertical elbow are genuinely distinct from the brown/green vocabulary used everywhere else, and the water source tank reads immediately as "a water source." This is the one zone where the color/material choice alone does real identity work without needing extra props. The ladder-into-a-pit structure on the left (E-cave-walkway, from Phase 6.5C2A) is visible mid-strip with its cool-tinted cap accent, legible even at this zoom.

**Strip 4 (x≈5200–7400, F hill → cave → descent).** The one genuinely different *traversal* in the game — a real ascent/descent silhouette instead of a flat walk between machines — but visually it is still built from the exact same brown land-mass + timber-post vocabulary as everywhere else. The moving platform (small grey plank, upper-middle) is easy to lose against the background at this zoom; at gameplay zoom it reads fine (confirmed in `r14-gua-songsang.jpg`), but it has no distinguishing color or outline, only its motion, to separate it from a static deck.

**Strip 5 (x≈7000–9200, F-cave exit → G river → destination).** The river is the one place the world commits to a large, uninterrupted *negative* space (water) instead of more land mass — visually the most effective single composition in the game, confirmed in `strip-05-x7000-9200.jpg` and `r15/r16`. The destination door (far right) is small relative to the screen and sits directly on ground level, not elevated or framed — it does not read as "the place you've been travelling 8400px to reach" the way a door at the top of a final rise, or set inside a frame, would. The deep repeating water-tile fill below the crossing (flagged already in Phase 6.5C1/6.5C2A as a known, deliberately low-priority issue) is visible here too — still present, still out of camera range during normal crossing, still not worth fixing on its own.

## Cross-world patterns (confirmed, not re-guessed)

1. **One structural module serves every role.** Posts+beam+ladder+timber-plank-deck is the vocabulary for: B's climb, B's lift frame, C's elevated deck, D's rack/service, E's source platform, F's step shelves, G's bank supports. This is efficient and never looks *broken*, but it is the single biggest reason the zones don't feel different from each other architecturally — confirmed by direct comparison across all five strips, not an assumption.
2. **Material/color is the only thing that successfully differentiates a zone** (E's blue pipes, G's water). Everywhere color doesn't do the work (A, B, D, F), the zones are only as different as their *puzzle*, not their *place*.
3. **The forest-silhouette background band (added Phase 6.5C2A) is present and consistent across all five strips with no visible seam or gap** — re-confirmed independently in this audit, not merely inherited from the 6.5C2B report.
4. **No zone has a true vertical focal point** — nothing in any strip is taller than its neighbors by more than one platform-tier; the eye has nowhere to be drawn to from a distance. Ori/Hollow Knight-style "silhouette you can read your position against from across the screen" (see file 02) does not exist anywhere in DENAI's current world.
