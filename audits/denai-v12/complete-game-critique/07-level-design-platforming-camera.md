# 07 — Level design, platforming, and camera

## A. Main route

**Navigation flow / route clarity:** Confirmed good. At every zone transition screenshotted (A→B, B→D, D→E, F→G), the next zone's key landmark is already visible at the edge of frame before the player arrives there (file 04, rooms R03/R07/R10/R13). This is deliberate forward-guidance and it works, matching the spirit of B1 (Mario's "teach, develop, twist, conclude" — the player is always shown what's coming before being asked to engage with it).

**Platform spacing / jump height-width:** `MOTION = {speed:280, acceleration:2100, braking:2500, jump:520, climb:150, coyote:110, buffer:130, bodyWidth:28, bodyHeight:60}` (confirmed from `src/level.js`). The one real jump gap in the game (`FIRST_GAP`, x=1080, width=108) is a modest 108px — well inside what a 520-velocity jump at 280 speed clears with margin (not independently computed here as a physics simulation, but consistent with the fact that no other gap in the entire 8400px world is wider, and the one gap that exists is explicitly flagged by its own source comment as a deliberately tuned "first gap" moment). **There is exactly one designed jump-over-a-gap in the whole game.** Every other "platforming" moment is climbing a ladder or walking up a short rise — not jumping.

**Landing safety / recovery after missed jumps:** Confirmed generous. `respawn()` has no damage, no life cost; the only fail states in the entire game are (a) falling below y=1510 (confirmed via source, a generous "fell off the bottom of everything" threshold) or (b) entering an unsolved gate's gap, or (c) Zone G's river specifically. Missing a jump anywhere in A-F just means walking back a few steps.

**Moving platform challenge:** One moving platform exists (F, `F_MOVING_PLATFORM`), optional (serves the cave route), with real riding physics (confirmed via `moving-platform.js`: velocity-matched horizontal carry while riding, correctly guards against double-applying dx). This is the single moving-target platforming challenge in an 8400px game.

**Height variation:** Confirmed limited. Checked every `SURFACES.y` value across the whole world: the full vertical range used for *walkable* ground is roughly y=738 (D-service, the single highest point) to y=1296 (lift's parked gate, not walkable) — in practical walkable terms, most of the game sits within a 300px band (y≈864-1188). F's hill is the only sustained multi-tier vertical traversal; everything else is one or two 54px steps at a time.

**Skill progression:** **None found.** `MOTION` constants are fixed and global — there is no later section of the game that asks for a harder jump, a longer gap, or a new movement skill than Zone A's own first gap. The game does not ask the player's platforming skill to grow at any point after the opening minutes.

**Repeated challenge patterns:** Confirmed, directly: the "walk to a sign, press E, read/select/test, repeat" loop appears in nearly identical shape in B (selector+lever), D (blocks+rack or console), E (two stage signs+lever), and G (sign+lever, twice). The *math* changes each time; the *physical verb* — approach, press E, read a result — does not.

**Empty traversal time:** Measured via room widths: R02 (540px, one prop at the far end), R07 (540px, now decorated), R10 (270px) are the longest stretches with no decision point. None are excessive by platformer standards, but combined with the single-tier terrain (above), they read as connective tissue rather than content.

**Rest areas:** The game has no dedicated "safe, nothing-to-do" rest beat distinct from its connective corridors — which is fine; a 10-15 minute level doesn't obviously need one, flagged only because the brief's challenge-rhythm checklist asks for it explicitly (see Part B below).

**Optional routes:** Three — C (elevated, puzzle-based), the crate ledge (A), F's cave (puzzle-based, inverse function). All three are genuinely optional (none gate the main route) and each rewards a secret or coin — this is a correctly-executed, standard "optional content that doesn't block progress" pattern (matches general secondary-source Mario-design writing cited as B2 in file 02).

**Checkpoint placement:** 14 checkpoints across 8400px (roughly one per 600px), several gated behind solved-gate requirements so a checkpoint can't be claimed "early" before the gate it depends on is actually passable — confirmed via `CHECKPOINTS`' `requires` field cross-checked against `sanitize()` in `save.js`, which independently re-derives and strips any checkpoint whose requirements aren't met, i.e., **the save system cannot be corrupted into an impossible checkpoint state even if localStorage is hand-edited.** This is unusually robust engineering for a student project and is worth calling out as a strength, not just absence of a bug.

**Does the game contain enough meaningful platforming?** **No — and this is the single most important Part 4A finding.** By any reasonable definition (a moment where the player must execute a non-trivial jump, under time pressure or precision demand, with a real chance of failure), DENAI has approximately one (`FIRST_GAP`) in the entire game. Per Celeste (A1/A2) and Super Meat Boy (E1/E2) — both built entirely around repeated, escalating platforming challenges — and per Mario's own "teach, develop, twist" structure (B1), which assumes the *mechanic being taught* is a platforming skill, DENAI's climbing/traversal is closer to "scenery to walk through between puzzles" than "the game's actual challenge." This is not automatically wrong (see the explicit brief instruction: "more platforms doesn't automatically mean more fun," and file 08's finding that DENAI's core loop is legitimately puzzle-first) — but it means DENAI should be evaluated and marketed as **an exploration/puzzle adventure with light platforming**, not as a platforming game with a puzzle layer, and several of the owner's stated concerns (route readability, structural repetition) matter *more*, not less, because the platforming itself isn't the main draw.

## B. Challenge rhythm — timeline across A→G

| Zone | Introduction | Safe experimentation | First challenge | Variation | Escalation | Recovery | Reward | Transition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Move/jump tutorial | Crate push (optional, no penalty) | The one real gap jump | — | — | Free retry, no damage | a-ledge coin (if solved) | Visible B ahead |
| B | Selector introduced | Cycle input freely before testing | Hit output=7 | — | — | Free retry | Checkpoint + bridge | D's rack visible ahead |
| C | Passive clue signs | Read both clues, no pressure | Apply/confirm | — | — | N/A (no fail state) | Secret + coin | Rejoins B |
| D | Number blocks | Any block testable freely | Hit g(x)=10 | Direct-press vs. console path (redundant, file 04) | — | Free retry (block returns to rack) | Checkpoint + bridge | E's tank visible ahead |
| E | Stage signs (F/G) | Choose order freely before activating | Hit output=19 | **Same decision shape as D's composition console two rooms earlier** | — | Free retry | Checkpoint + bridge | F's hill visible |
| F | — (pure traversal) | — | Climbing itself | Cave detour (inverse logic) | **The only real escalation in the game: inverse reasoning, not forward computation** | N/A | f-ridge / f-cave-gem | G's bank visible |
| G | Two-lock sign+lever | Cycle input/order freely | Lock 1 (solve h(x)=17) | Lock 2 (compare f/g order) | **Bigger numbers (4x-3 vs 2x+1), same decision shape as E/D** | Free retry; river fall = real respawn | Bridge + ending | Destination |

**Findings, stated plainly:**

- **The escalation curve is nearly flat.** Every zone's "first challenge" is structurally the same move (pick an input/order, test, read output, repeat on failure) applied to a different equation. The one place real escalation happens is F's optional inverse cave — which is *optional*, meaning a player who skips it experiences zero escalation in difficulty or thinking-mode across the entire ~15-minute game.
- **G's finale is explicitly flagged by the brief as a concern, and the concern is justified:** Lock 1 (h(x)=4x-3=17) is a harder-number version of B/D's single-function solve; Lock 2 (compare f/g order) is the *exact same decision* as D's composition console (R10) and E's waterworks setup, just with different target numbers. The finale does not introduce a new kind of thinking — it re-runs two already-seen decision types back-to-back. This matches a real risk the brief asks to check for directly.
- **"Too many passive corridors"**: Confirmed at a specific, countable level — R02, R07, R09, R10 (four of sixteen rooms) have no decision point at all, only walking. Combined with the flat escalation above, roughly a quarter of the game's rooms contribute pure traversal time with no variation in verb.
- **Repeated easy jumps:** Not found as a pattern (there's only one jump-gap total, as established in Part A) — the repetition problem is in the *puzzle* verb, not the *platforming* verb.
- **Sudden difficulty spikes:** None found — if anything the opposite (near-total flatness) is the issue.
- **Overloaded puzzle clusters:** None found — each zone has exactly one puzzle, never stacked.
- **Unrewarding detours:** Not found — every optional route (C, crate ledge, F-cave) gives a real coin and/or secret.
- **Missing moments of surprise:** The split-hinge lever (G) and the animated water-flow dot (E) are the two genuine "oh, that's a nice touch" moments found in this audit (file 06/12) — both are late in the game (E is roughly 55% through, G is the finale). A→D has no equivalent moment.

**A better progression curve, if pursued (design direction only, not implemented — this is a read-only audit):** Move one piece of "escalation" earlier — e.g., make D's composition console a genuinely different decision from E's waterworks (not just the same order-comparison again), or front-load a taste of inverse reasoning (F's optional mechanic) somewhere non-optional earlier, even in a trivial form, so G's finale reads as "the hardest version of something I've done several different ways" rather than "the same thing, bigger numbers." This is explored further in files 09 and 16.

## C. Camera and viewport

- **Follow behavior:** `cameras.main.startFollow(player, true, .14, .16, -85, 10)` — lerped follow with a slight forward offset (85px ahead in the player's facing direction... actually confirmed as a fixed -85 x-offset, not facing-direction-aware, per source) and a 10px downward offset. Smooth, no jarring snaps observed in any capture.
- **Vertical camera changes:** Bounds are `setBounds(0,300,BATCH_END,1566-300)` — confirmed the camera cannot scroll above world y=300, which (per file 05) is exactly why the background band's fixed placement at y=430 never clips.
- **Sightlines before jumps:** Confirmed good for the one real gap (R03, file 04) — both sides of the gap are in frame together before the jump is required.
- **Hidden landing areas:** None found — every drop-down (D-service, E-recovery, F-cave) was visible in its own room's capture without requiring a blind jump.
- **What players can see ahead:** Consistently good (see Part A's "forward-guidance" finding) — this is a genuine strength, not a gap.
- **Camera occlusion by UI:** This is where real issues exist, but they belong to file 11 (UI/UX), not camera mechanics themselves — the camera is never the cause; the HTML overlay panels (`#mechanism`, `#b/c/e/g-control`) are plain absolutely-positioned DOM elements layered on top of the canvas, not camera-aware, so they can sit over gameplay real estate regardless of where the camera is pointed. Confirmed directly in R05/R11/R15 captures: the world-aside panels occupy roughly the top third of the screen whenever active.
- **Portrait vs. landscape:** Not independently screenshot-verified at a true resized viewport in this audit (the browser automation environment could not resize the actual window — see file 11 for the full disclosure). Reasoned directly from CSS media queries instead (`presentation.css`'s `@media(max-height:560px) and (orientation:landscape)` block, confirmed to restructure `#mechanism` into a two-column grid for landscape mobile) — this is a real, deliberate adaptation, just not independently photographed.

**No camera rule changes are recommended by this audit** (per the brief's explicit instruction not to touch camera rules) — the camera itself is not where this game's visual/compositional problems live.

## D. Movement / game feel

Code-derived facts, clearly separated from anything requiring human play-feel judgment:

- **Coyote time (110ms) and jump buffering (130ms) both exist and are correctly implemented** (confirmed: `time-this.jumpAt<MOTION.buffer && time-this.groundedAt<MOTION.coyote`) — this is the exact A1 (Celeste) forgiveness pattern, already present in DENAI without anyone needing to add it. **This is a genuine, evidence-based strength worth stating explicitly** — the owner's brief doesn't mention movement feel as a complaint, and this audit found nothing wrong with it.
- **Acceleration/braking are asymmetric and tuned** (2100 accel / 2500 brake, both modestly higher than top speed 280, giving snappy-but-not-instant starts and stops) — code-derived, consistent with "responsive but not twitchy" platformer feel; whether it *feels* that way in practice needs human playtesting (file 13), not asserted here as fact.
- **Jump arc/height (520 velocity) is fixed and un-variable** (no variable jump height by hold-duration found in the update loop) — a deliberate simplicity choice consistent with the "one designed jump in the whole game" finding in Part A; not a defect for this game's actual demands, would be a limitation if more platforming were added.
- **Air control:** Full horizontal acceleration is available in the air (no separate air-acceleration constant found, confirmed the same `change` formula applies whether `grounded` or not) — generous, player-friendly, consistent with a game that isn't testing precision air control.
- **Ladder responsiveness:** Dedicated climb state with its own velocity scale (`MOTION.climb=150`), exits correctly via jump-off (confirmed) or walking off either end (confirmed, with a 180-250ms lock to prevent instant re-grab) — no issues found.
- **Moving-platform boarding:** Confirmed correct velocity-carry logic with an explicit comment addressing a real Arcade-physics double-apply pitfall (`moving-platform.js`) — this is careful, correct engineering, not guessed-at.
- **Character animation:** Four states (idle/run/jump/fall) with a dedicated fix (documented in a source comment) for a previously-real flash-of-wrong-pose bug at jump apex and ledge-walk-off — confirmed the fix's logic is sound (airborne states are now velocity-based, not grounded-check-based). Squash/stretch on takeoff/landing, scaled by impact velocity, with a reduced-motion fallback. **This is more game-feel polish than the brief's visual complaints would suggest exists** — worth the owner knowing movement animation is not a weak point.
- **Landing feedback:** Dust particles scaled by fall speed, a landing SFX call scaled by impact — both confirmed present in source, not independently heard/seen (audio playback wasn't verified in this audit; see file 12).
- **Interaction feedback:** A pulsing glow ring appears on any nearby interactable (terminal or secret), using sine-wave alpha under normal motion and a flat alpha under reduced-motion — confirmed present and correctly reduced-motion-aware.
- **Touch input responsiveness:** Not independently tested on a real touch device in this audit (no physical/emulated touch input was exercised — only keyboard and direct scene API calls). CSS confirms touch targets are ≥44px (`min-width:44px;min-height:44px` throughout `presentation.css`/`world.css`), a real accessibility-conscious choice, but actual touch *feel* (latency, hold-to-repeat behavior for `data-hold` buttons) needs human playtesting, not asserted here.

**What needs human playtesting (not claimed as verified fact anywhere in this file):** overall movement "feel" (speed/weight subjective judgment), touch control responsiveness on a real device, and whether the flat difficulty curve (Part B) is actually boring in practice or merely *measured* as flat — these are exactly the questions file 13's playtest plan is built to answer.
